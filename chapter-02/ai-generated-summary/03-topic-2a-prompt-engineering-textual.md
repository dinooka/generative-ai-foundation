# Topic 2A: Prompt Engineering for Textual Information

> Study notes summarized in our own words. Not verbatim course text. Single scrollable page.

## Key points
- Specific beats vague: precise prompts with context produce better text; adding **constraints** (format, tone, length, audience) sharpens results.
- Prompt syntax guidance: keep prompts clear, not too short or too long ("Goldilocks" length); state the task and set the tone; minor spelling/grammar slips are usually still understood.
- Tokenization insight: the model reads **tokens** (word fragments), not whole words, and predicts based on patterns.
- Content-gathering prompt types: fact-finding, statistical, comparison, cause-and-effect, exploratory, and multi-step prompts.
- Six tips for better prompts: be specific, provide context, break complex questions down, add constraints, invite multiple perspectives, request step-by-step explanations.
- Intermediate techniques: challenge-based prompts, feedback requests, role-playing, expert emulation, **negative prompts** (stating what to avoid), custom models.
- Summarization: general, detailed, focused, and comparative summaries; alternative trigger words (condense, paraphrase, outline, brief, simplify, recap, highlight, abstract); watch for oversimplification and misinterpretation.
- Creative prompting: new concepts, storytelling/character development, poems/songs, what-if scenarios, style and tone, imagery, and creative constraints.
- Content transformation (domain 2.2): reformatting, editing/proofreading (e.g., AI writing assistants), visualization (bullets, headlines, flowcharts), changing media type, translating, and personalizing/adapting content.
- Embedded knowledge checks appear after sections.
