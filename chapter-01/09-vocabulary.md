# Chapter 1: Vocabulary

> Study notes summarized in our own words. Not verbatim course text.
> The in-course item is an interactive 62-card flashcard deck; the cards below are the chapter's vocabulary drawn from the official Key Terms list and quiz vocabulary questions.

- **Artificial Intelligence (AI)** — the broad field of building systems that perform tasks requiring human-like intelligence.
- **Machine Learning** — an AI subset where systems improve by learning patterns from data rather than explicit programming.
- **Generative AI** — AI that creates new content (text, images, audio, video) from learned patterns instead of just analyzing existing data.
- **Predictive AI** — AI that forecasts future outcomes based on historical data.
- **Discriminative AI** — AI that sorts inputs into categories by learning boundaries between classes.
- **Analytical AI** — AI that uncovers relationships and trends in complex datasets.
- **Statistical AI** — AI applying mathematical tools like regression and probability to support decisions.
- **Transformer Models** — neural architectures that weigh relationships between sequence elements; the basis of modern language models.
- **Diffusion Models** — generative models that rebuild coherent data from random noise; common in image generation.
- **VAE (Variational Autoencoder)** — a model that compresses data to a compact form and reconstructs variations of it.
- **GAN** — two neural networks (generator vs. discriminator) competing to produce realistic synthetic outputs.
- **CNN** — layered networks specialized in understanding image components; used for classification.
- **Tokenization** — splitting text into small units (words, parts, punctuation) for model processing.
- **Token** — the basic unit a language model predicts when generating text.
- **Prompt** — the instruction or question given to an AI model to guide its output.
- **Input** — any data supplied to the model (text, image, audio, video).
- **Output** — the content the model generates in response.
- **Parameter** — an adjustable setting (length, style, format, tone) shaping the output.
- **Customization** — tailoring a model with fixed instructions, examples, approved files, or workflows for specific needs.
- **Refinement / Refinement Algorithms** — techniques that fine-tune or improve AI-generated content.
- **Refinement Steps** — the iterative check-and-adjust loop used to improve model results.
- **Multimodal Generation** — producing advanced content by combining multiple input types or models.
- **Hallucinations** — plausible-sounding but false information invented by the model.
- **Bias** — skewed outputs reflecting human assumptions or underrepresented groups in training data.
- **Omission** — gaps where the training data lacked coverage of a topic or group.
- **Artifacts** — visual glitches/errors in AI-generated images.
- **Critical Thinking** — questioning and evaluating AI content instead of accepting it at face value.
- **Accessibility** — how available AI tools are, depending on infrastructure, cost, and connectivity.
- **Moore's Law** — the observation that computing power roughly doubles about every two years.
- **Model Drift** — shifts in a model's responses after updates or retraining.
- **Supervised Learning** — training on labeled data with known correct answers.
- **Unsupervised Learning** — finding structure/clusters in unlabeled data.
- **Reinforcement Learning** — learning through trial and error guided by rewards and penalties.
- **Pre-training** — the initial large-scale training phase on vast datasets.
- **Inference** — the stage where a trained model generates outputs from new inputs.
