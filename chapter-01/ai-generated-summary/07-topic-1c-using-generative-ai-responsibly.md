# Topic 1C: Using Generative AI Responsibly

> Study notes summarized in our own words. Not verbatim course text. Single scrollable page.

## Key points
- Output is not fully reliable:
  - **Hallucinations** — polished but invented information.
  - **Bias** — training-data patterns underrepresenting cultures/professions.
  - **Omission** — gaps in training data.
  - **Artifacts** — visual errors in image models (e.g., spatial/depth errors).
- Consistency limits: identical prompts can yield different results because generation is probabilistic; image outputs may show mismatched shadows or shifting character details — a problem for professional workflows.
- Infrastructure demands: large-scale computing, data centers, high-speed internet, capable hardware/cloud access; availability varies with infrastructure.
- Privacy and governance concerns: data collection practices, deep-fake scams, data breaches, and the lack of universal standards.
- Rapid change: **model drift** (responses shifting after updates) and fast obsolescence require ongoing learning.
- Human expertise remains central: domain knowledge directs and validates output; computing power keeps growing (**Moore's Law**), but experts steer the tools.
- Embedded knowledge checks appear after sections.

hallucinations, bias, omission, artifacts, consistency, privacy, accessibility, model drift, Moore's Law, critical thinking.
