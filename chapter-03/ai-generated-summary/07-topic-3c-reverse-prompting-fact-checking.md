# Topic 3C: Reverse Prompting and Fact-Checking

> Study notes summarized in our own words. Not verbatim course text. Single scrollable page.

## Key points
- **Reverse prompting:** tell the AI precisely what to avoid — guardrails/negative prompts constrain output while leaving room for creativity.
- Useful exclusion phrases: avoid mentioning, do not include, exclude references, keep tone, steer clear, focus on, leave out, make sure not to, restrict content to, do not use examples from.
- Applications: content control, tone and style management, specificity/focus, and creative uses.
- Effective reverse prompts: weak versions are vague; strong versions list broad exclusions first, then specifics; order matters, and trial-and-error iteration improves results.
- **Fact-checking** (domain 3.5): verify historical facts, current facts, and numerical data separately.
- **Cross-referencing:** consult multiple reliable sources — academic journals, reputable news, official sites, authoritative bodies (World Bank/IMF, National Weather Service, Census Bureau).
- **Historical facts:** beware plausible-but-wrong claims, nuanced events, and biased accounts of controversial topics; use primary documents and expert analyses.
- **Current facts:** challenges include constant change and misinformation; prefer established news outlets, official sites, and fact-checking organizations; check URL/domain, author credentials, date, clickbait signs, and site purpose.
- **Numerical data:** rely on official statistics, research papers, and financial reports; verify source credibility, cross-check multiple sources, watch for red flags and ranges vs. estimates; basic math literacy (percentages, averages, ratios, estimation) helps.
- Reasoning tools: **deductive reasoning** (general to specific) and **inductive reasoning** (specific observations to general conclusions).
