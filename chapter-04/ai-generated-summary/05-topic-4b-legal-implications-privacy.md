# Topic 4B: Legal Implications and Privacy

> Study notes summarized in our own words. Not verbatim course text. Single scrollable page.

## Key points
- AI-generated work complicates intellectual property because it is built from existing data; IP rights protect books, music, artwork, and inventions.
- Training-data copyright: models may have been trained on protected material without permission; consequences include fines and lawsuits; using public-domain content is a safer path.
- Laws are still evolving and vary by country — apply the same caution you would with non-AI content and do not use others' work without rights.
- **Transparency:** documenting your AI process (e.g., disclosing AI use in product descriptions) builds trust and accountability, even where not legally required.
- **Privacy:** personal or company data fed into models may become training data; PII exposure enables identity theft, with civil and criminal consequences; companies now set internal policies against leaking data to public/unapproved tools; human-created content may train models unless you opt out.
- Other legal risks: liability for false or misleading AI content, mishandling personal information, and collecting more data than needed.
- Regulatory examples: **GDPR** (EU data protection), **CCPA** (California consumer data), **HIPAA** (healthcare data).
- Misuse examples: synthetic identities, blackmail, and a CEO deepfake scam illustrate how generated media enables fraud.
- Security note: models learn from the open internet, so data fragments can leak and travel beyond your control — consider who really controls the information.
- Case study: an AI-generated marketing campaign — recommended steps are checking copyright concerns, seeking legal advice, revising/customizing output, checking sentiment, and issuing a disclaimer.
